## Construction details with numbers

**FSK duct wrap (external)**
- The laminate is foil (outside), then glass scrim, then kraft, flame-retardant bonded. Wrap is commonly 1½–3 in thick blanket at about 0.75–1.5 pcf (typical catalog range).
- It is installed facing out with a **2 in (51 mm) stapling flange** at each seam: one edge has 2 in of fiberglass stripped off the facing.
- The flange is stapled with **outward-clinch staples about 6 in (150 mm) o.c.**, then taped with matching **FSK tape, 3 in wide typical**.
- On ducts **≥ 24 in wide**, the bottom is also secured with **pins and speed washers at about 18 in o.c.** to stop sag.
- The wrap is sized with a "stretch-out" that allows about 25% compression at the corners. Expect visibly thinner, rounded corners and pillowed faces in between.
- Sources: Knauf Performance+ Duct Wrap and CertainTeed SoftTouch spec sheets, the SOFTR Duct Wrap guide, https://conklinmetal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Knauf-Performance-Plus-Duct-Wrap-Spec-Sheet.pdf and https://cdn.bakerdist.com/softr-duct-wrap-frk-ii.pdf

**Duct liner (internal) pin layout** (Owens Corning guide spec, citing SMACNA HVAC-DCS)

| Velocity | From transverse (leading) edge of liner | From duct corners | Across width (o.c.) | Along length (o.c.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2,500 fpm | 3 in | 4 in | 12 in | 18 in |
| 2,501–5,000 fpm (one OC doc says 6,000) | 3 in | 4 in | 6 in | 16 in |

- Ducts 8 in wide or less inside need no pins on that face (Hercules layout sheet).
- Pins go 3 in from each side of a butted longitudinal liner seam that is away from a corner.
- Metal nosing is required on leading edges where unlined metal comes before the liner, and on all leading edges above 4,000 fpm.
- Liner is typically adhered with about 90% adhesive coverage plus pins. That figure is general NAIMA/SMACNA practice; I did not re-verify it this session.
- Fasteners must **not compress the liner more than ⅛ in** (Owens Corning QuietR spec).

**Pins and washers**
- **Weld pins**: capacitor-discharge 10- or 12-gauge pins, copper-coated steel. The washer is round and cupped, **about 1 in diameter**, 0.015–0.017 in thick (Hercules).
- **Self-adhesive or perforated-base pins**: 12-ga pins on about 2 × 2 in plates.
- **Speed washers (self-locking)**: about **1½ in round or square**, 0.016 in galvanized, beveled edge.
- The pin is cut or bent over after the washer goes on.
- On 1 in liner, pins are often ¾ in long, so the washer sits flush or slightly dimpled.

**Duct board**
- Duct board is 1–2 in rigid fiberglass with FSK outside and a coated airstream inside. It is fabricated with shiplap or V-groove folds.
- Seams are closed with **outward-clinch staples about 2 in o.c. on the flaps, plus UL 181A-P foil tape** (typical NAIMA fabrication; I did not verify this against a specific source in this session).

**MICA**
- MICA Plate **3-120** (7th edition) covers "exposed duct insulation". It says board goes on rectangular ducts and wrap-around or pipe-type insulation on round and oval ducts. Outdoor work needs a weather-barrier jacket over the factory facing.
- The plate is hosted at https://www.wbdg.org/files/pdfs/MICA_3-120.pdf. That URL serves a bot-check page to scripts, so I did not download it and only read its text through a search index.

## Visual / PBR guidance (sRGB)

- **FSK foil face.** Base **`#D9DBDD`**, metallic 1, roughness 0.25–0.40.
  - Add a **crinkle normal** (random 5–20 mm wrinkles) plus a **scrim grid** of raised lines at about 12–25 mm pitch, often tri-directional (a triangle and diamond look).
  - Use large-scale pillowing between staple lines.
  - In photos it samples about `#BBBABA` under fluorescent light.
- **Foil tape.** Brighter and flatter than the facing: roughness 0.15–0.25, no scrim, 3 in wide, sometimes slightly wrinkled.
- **Duct liner airstream face.** Black acrylic coating, base `#1E1F1F`, roughness 0.6–0.75, with a fine glass-mat fiber normal.
- **Washers.** Galvanized, base `#A8ACAE`, metallic 1, roughness 0.45, with spangle optional.
